## 3. Recurring difficulties

1. **Subject–verb agreement slips under load — the most consistent error.** Four on 2026-08-27: *wir muss*, *wir hat*, *Her* for *Er*, *sprechen* for *spricht*. Plus *sie gehst* in session 7. Everything complicated in the sentence is right; the verb form is the casualty. A checking habit, not a knowledge gap.
2. **Word order in the middle field.** TeMoLo (Time → Manner → Place) wrong in ex. 22, 23, 30 — but correct unprompted in 27, 29, and 32 (all three slots). Holds when the time phrase is short and obvious; slips when the place phrase feels central.
3. **Feminine accusative article/adjective endings** — *eine günstigen* → *eine günstige*. The *-en* default over-applied to one of the five exception slots. Masculine and neuter are already solid.
4. **Gender feeding the contractions** — *zur Arzt* for *zum Arzt* (masc.), and *zur Supermarkt* in session 7. The contractions themselves are fixed; the gender behind them breaks.
5. **Fixed prepositions** — *Angst von* → *Angst vor*. Welded to the verb/noun, not derivable from meaning.
6. **Vocabulary retrieval failures repeating across sessions** — *sich verabschieden*, *sich beeilen*, *wiedersehen*, *das Fenster*, *das Essen*. Supplied rather than produced.
7. **Preposition vs. conjunction** — *nach*/*nachdem*, *vor*/*bevor*. Wrote *Bevor ins Bett gehen*. Still undrilled.
8. **English bleeding in under load** — *Her*, *We*, *Munich*.
9. **sein vs. haben in the Perfekt** for change-of-state verbs.
10. **Konjunktiv II der Vergangenheit** — infinitive instead of past participle.

## 8. Format that works

- **One exercise at a time**, not batches — explicitly requested.
- Sets of **20 items**, worked through sequentially.
- **Corrections requested explicitly.**
- **English prompts for zu / um…zu must use plain "to", never "in order to"** — otherwise the English cues the construction.
- Commit progress mid-session on request. GitHub writes require Gonçalo's approval each time; reads do not.
- Seeding exercises with low-scoring vocabulary from `vocabulary.md` works well — it surfaced *verlassen*, *helfen*, *liegen*, *sich treffen*, and *die Besprechung* without a separate vocabulary drill.
- **Explaining the underlying system on request lands well.** Strong/weak verbs and the ein-word ending table were both asked for mid-session and both produced immediate correct output (ex. 36, ex. 38–39). Worth offering the mechanism rather than only the correction.
