# ARTICLE AND ADJECTIVE ENDINGS — opened 2026-08-27 at Gonçalo's explicit request

He asked for more drilling on this after ex. 37 (*ein kleinen Tisch*). Treat as a live topic.

## ein-words (ein, kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr)

| | masc | neut | fem | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nom** | ein | ein | eine | keine |
| **Akk** | ein**en** | ein | eine | keine |
| **Dat** | ein**em** | ein**em** | ein**er** | kein**en** |

Only the masculine changes between Nominativ and Akkusativ. That single *-en* is where most errors live.

## Adjective endings after an ein-word

The adjective picks up whatever the article failed to carry. **-en is the default**; only five slots deviate:
- masc nom → *ein klein**er** Tisch*
- neut nom/akk → *ein klein**es** Haus*
- fem nom/akk → *eine klein**e** Wohnung*
- everything else → **-en**: *einen kleinen, einem kleinen, einer kleinen, mit kleinen Kindern*

**Results 2026-08-27:**
- Ex. 37: *ein kleinen Tisch* → **einen kleinen Tisch**. Adjective right, article wrong — the reverse of the usual slip.
- Ex. 38: *einen kleinen Bruder* and *ein altes Auto* — **both correct first time**, the two hardest slots (masc akk marked; neut akk bare, adjective carries *-es*).
- Ex. 39: *einer jungen Frau* and *einen neuen Film* — both correct, different cases in one sentence.
- Ex. 40: *eine günstigen Wohnung* → **eine günstige**. The *-en* default over-applied to feminine accusative, which is one of the five exceptions. Feminine and neuter never change between Nom and Akk; only masculine does.

Pattern: the masculine and neuter are solid; **feminine accusative is the weak slot**.

# PREPOSITIONS

## Always Dativ, no decision needed

**aus, außer, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu** — never take any other case. The only question is *which* preposition.

**bei** — the employer/premises preposition: *bei der Firma*, *bei Siemens*, *beim Arzt* (at the doctor's) vs. *zum Arzt* (going there), *bei mir* (at my place). Correct unaided in ex. 30.

## Obligatory contractions — zur vs. zum, ERROR 2026-08-27

*von + dem* → **vom** · *zu + dem* → **zum** · *zu + der* → **zur** · *an + dem* → **am** · *in + dem* → **im** · *bei + dem* → **beim** · *an + das* → **ans** · *in + das* → **ins**

- **zur** = *zu + der* → FEMININE only
- **zum** = *zu + dem* → masculine and neuter

Ex. 29: wrote *zur Arzt* → **zum Arzt**. Same slip as *zur Supermarkt* in session 7. The contractions themselves are fixed (*vom, zum, am* all correct unprompted); it is the gender feeding them that breaks.

## aus vs. von — RESOLVED 2026-08-27

- **aus** = emerging from *inside* an enclosed space: *aus dem Haus, aus der Tasche, aus dem Zug*
- **von** = away from a point or surface: *vom Bahnhof, vom Tisch*

Wrong in ex. 28 (*vom Hause*), then **correct unprompted in ex. 31** (*aus der Tasche*). Fixed in three exercises.

## Two-way prepositions (an, auf, in, über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen) — NOW A STRENGTH

Dativ for location (*wo?*), Akkusativ for movement (*wohin?*). **The verb decides.**

- Ex. 25: *liegen auf dem Tisch* — static, Dativ. Correct.
- Ex. 31: *legen auf **dem** Tisch* → **auf den Tisch**. Movement, Akkusativ. Same preposition and noun as ex. 25, different verb, and the case did not switch.
- Ex. 34: *vor dem Haus* (static, Dativ) and *warten auf ihren Bruder* (fixed prep, Akkusativ) in one sentence. Both correct.
- **Ex. 36: *an die Wand gehängt* (Akk.) and *über dem Sofa* (Dat.) in ONE sentence, both correct unaided.** The hardest item given so far.
- Ex. 37: *zwischen **die** Fenstern* → **zwischen den Fenstern**. Location, so Dativ.

## Fixed prepositions — welded to the verb/noun, not to the meaning

- **Angst haben vor + Dat.** — ERROR ex. 33: wrote *Angst von* → **vor**. Case was right; preposition wrong.
- **sich fürchten vor + Dat.** — same meaning, more formal
- **warten auf + Akk.** — correct ex. 12 and again ex. 34
- **sprechen über + Akk.** — correct ex. 39
- **sich freuen auf + Akk.**, **denken an + Akk.**

Separating the two that blur:
- **von** + Dat. = from, of — *vom Bahnhof*, *ein Freund von mir*
- **vor** + Dat. = before, in front of, and in fear expressions — *vor dem Haus*, *vor dem Essen*, *Angst vor*

## Dative plural adds -n to the NOUN

Whenever the plural doesn't already end in *-n* or *-s*: *mit den Freunden, mit den Kindern, aus den Häusern, zwischen den Fenstern*.
Correct unaided in ex. 32 (*mit Freunden*) and ex. 37 (*Fenstern*).

## Countries and cities

**nach** for countries without an article and all cities: *nach Italien, nach München*. Exceptions take *in + Akk.*: *in die Schweiz, in die Türkei, in die USA*.

## Time prepositions

- **um** — clock times: *um acht Uhr* (Akkusativ)
- **am** — days and parts of day: *am Montag, am Abend*
- **im** — months and seasons: *im Juli, im Sommer*
- **seit** — duration up to now, + Dativ: *seit letztem Jahr*, *seit März* (bare noun for months)
- bare — *nächste Woche, jeden Tag*

All correct across ex. 21–40.

# VERBS

## Strong / weak / mixed — explained 2026-08-27 at Gonçalo's request

- **Weak** = regular. Stem never changes: *legen – legte – hat gelegt*. Pattern: **-te** past, **ge- + stem + -t** participle.
- **Strong** = irregular. Stem vowel shifts: *liegen – lag – hat gelegen*. Participle ends in **-en**.
- **Mixed** (~8 verbs): vowel change + weak endings — *bringen/brachte/gebracht*, *denken/dachte/gedacht*.

No rule predicts which verbs are strong; they are the old high-frequency ones, ~200 total. Every new verb entering German is weak.

**Why it matters here:** in the static/movement pairs the static verb is always STRONG and the movement verb always WEAK. If the participle is *gelegt*, it is the movement verb, which means Akkusativ. **The verb form tells you the case.**

## Static / movement pairs

| Static (strong) | Movement (weak) |
|---|---|
| liegen – lag – gelegen | legen – legte – gelegt |
| sitzen – saß – gesessen | setzen – setzte – gesetzt |
| stehen – stand – gestanden | stellen – stellte – gestellt |
| hängen – hing – gehangen | hängen – hängte – gehängt |

**hängen** is both halves of its own pair and changes conjugation to signal which. Ex. 36 used **gehängt** (weak) for the action and drove *an die Wand* to Akkusativ correctly.

# GRAMMAR NOTES

## Word order — IMPROVING but still the main problem

**Middle-field order: Time → Manner → Place (TeMoLo).**
*Ich fahre **morgen mit dem Zug nach Berlin**.*

| Ex. | Result |
|---|---|
| 22 | wrong — *in München seit letztem Jahr* |
| 23 | wrong — *zum Arzt mit seinem Bruder* |
| 27 | **correct unprompted** — *um acht am Bahnhof* |
| 29 | **correct unprompted** — *mit meiner Mutter zum Arzt* |
| 30 | wrong — *bei dieser Firma seit März* |
| 32 | **correct unprompted, all three slots** — *im Sommer mit Freunden nach Italien* |

Pattern: holds when the time expression is short and obvious (*um acht*, *im Sommer*); slips when the place phrase feels more central to the sentence.

**Pronoun objects move left**, ahead of adverbs and time expressions. Wrong ex. 2 and ex. 20, correct ex. 26.

**Fronted phrase vs. fronted clause — comma rule**
- **Phrase** (no verb) → NO comma: *Nach der Besprechung haben wir…*
- **Clause** (has a verb) → comma: *Nachdem wir gesprochen hatten, sind wir gegangen.*
Wrong ex. 24, then **correct unprompted in ex. 29 and ex. 35**. Fixed.

**Fronted element → finite verb second.** SOLID across three sessions, including in the second clause of a compound sentence (ex. 36).

## Preposition vs. conjunction — the same word twice

- **nach** = preposition + Dativ → *nach dem Essen*
- **nachdem** = conjunction + full clause, verb last
- **vor** = preposition + Dativ → *vor dem Haus*
- **bevor** = conjunction + full clause → *bevor sie ins Bett ging*

Error ex. 19 (*Bevor ins Bett gehen*). English blurs both. Still worth a dedicated drill.

## zu-placement — THE TEST THAT WORKS

Does the prefix split off in the present tense? Separable → **zu inside** (*abzuholen*). Inseparable → **zu in front** (*zu verabschieden*). No prefix → *zu* in front.

**STATUS: 11/11 correct on 2026-08-27.** Was the top recurring error; now the strongest area. Re-test in a later session.

## The zu-family

- **um…zu / statt…zu / ohne…zu** behave identically: comma, no subject, *zu*-infinitive at the end. SOLID.
- **plain zu** when a verb or noun demands the infinitive; **um…zu** for genuine purpose (test: does "in order to" sound natural).
- **zu + adjective ALWAYS → um…zu**: *zu müde, um heute zu kochen*. Regressed ex. 10 but correctly distinguished from the bare adjective in ex. 15.
- **würde + BARE infinitive** — no *zu*.
- **Da-compounds**: *darauf, darüber, damit, dafür, davon, daran*. Opened 2026-08-26, **STILL NOT DRILLED**.

## Verb forms

- **Subject–verb agreement slips under load — FOUR on 2026-08-27**: *wir muss*, *wir hat*, *Her* for *Er*, *sprechen* for *spricht*. Plus *sie gehst* in session 7. Consistent shape: everything complicated in the sentence is right and the verb form is the casualty. Not a knowledge gap — a checking habit. Reread subject and verb before submitting.
- **English bleeding in under load**: *Her* for *Er* (ex. 31), *We*/*Munich* in session 7.
- **müssen**: irregular singular, regular plural.
- **Modals: umlaut = hypothesis.** Präteritum loses it; Konjunktiv II keeps it.
- **Ersatzinfinitiv — SOLID.** **hätte + main verb infinitive + modal infinitive**, including the sein-verb exception.
- **es gibt** — forces Akkusativ on what follows. Correct ex. 37. German prefers **stehen** for objects positioned in space: *Zwischen den zwei Fenstern **steht** ein kleiner Tisch.*
- **zwei** (the number) vs. **zweiten** (second) — confused in ex. 37.
